Caldera — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 29 September 2026  ·  Last updated: 2 October 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how information is handled when you play Caldera (the "Game"), a survival shooter for Android in which you fly a drone up a shaft ahead of rising lava. "We", "us" and "our" mean the Game's developer. By using the Game, you agree to the practices described here.

Questions? Contact us at support@abduloski.dev.

The short version

  • There is no account and no sign-in. We never ask for your name or email.
  • Your progress and settings stay on your device. We run no server of our own.
  • We use Firebase Analytics to see how the Game is played and Firebase Crashlytics to find crashes. We never set a user ID.
  • The Game shows ads through Google AdMob, which uses your device's advertising ID. Rewarded ads only play when you choose to watch one.
  • Players in Europe and the UK are asked before personalised ads are shown, and can change their answer in Settings → Ad Preferences.
  • In-game purchases go through Google Play. We never see your card or payment details.
  • The Game never asks for your location, camera, microphone, contacts or files, and never shows a permission prompt.
  • We do not sell your personal information.

1. Who we are

Caldera is an independently developed Android game by Haris Abduloski, distributed through Google Play. We are the data controller for the limited information described in this policy. Google acts as an independent controller for the advertising data it collects through its own SDK (see section 2b). You can reach us at support@abduloski.dev.

2. Information we collect and why

We do not run a server and the Game has no account system. Limited information leaves your device through the Google services used for analytics, crash reports and advertising, as described below. We can view aggregated Analytics reports and crash reports in Firebase, but we never receive your name, your email address or anything else that directly identifies you.

a. Analytics and crash reports

We use Google Analytics for Firebase to understand how the Game is played and whether it works. Firebase automatically records information such as app launches, sessions, app and operating-system version, device model, approximate country or region, the Google Play campaign or link the Game was installed from (if any), and a Firebase app-instance identifier. Firebase Analytics may also collect your device's advertising ID.

We also record a fixed set of game events:

  • when a run starts: which drone you fly, and whether you use touch or joystick controls;
  • when a run ends: whether you fell, escaped or quit, how long the run lasted, the souls earned, the level and zone reached, the height climbed, whether you used a Continue, and what destroyed your drone;
  • when you defeat a boss: which boss, and how many seconds the fight took;
  • when you pick an upgrade card: which card, whether it came from a level-up or a chest, and your level;
  • when you spend souls on a drone or a permanent upgrade: the item, its new rank and its price in souls;
  • when an ad is shown: which kind, whether it was watched to the end and, for a full-screen ad, the screen change that showed it.

These events contain game information only. They never contain your name, email address, location or any other personal details, and the Game never sets a Firebase user ID. Each copy of the Game also reports whether it is a test build or the released version, so that test play can be kept out of the reports.

We use Firebase Crashlytics to find and fix crashes, including crashes in the game engine itself. A crash report can include a stack trace, the time and state of the Game at the crash, app and operating-system version, device model, available memory and storage, and a Crashlytics installation identifier. Crashlytics also counts app sessions, so it can tell how often the Game runs without crashing. We do not set a user ID or attach anything else to these reports.

Google processes this information under Google's Privacy Policy and Firebase privacy and security terms. Analytics and crash reports apply to every player. The consent form described in section 2b covers advertising.

b. Advertising

The Game shows ads supplied by Google AdMob:

  • Rewarded ads, which only play when you choose to watch one: when your drone is destroyed you can watch an ad to continue the run, and on the results screen you can watch one to double the souls the run earned. Each is offered at most once per run.
  • Full-screen ads when you move between screens outside of play, for example when you open the Shop or leave the results screen. They are never shown during a run, they are spaced several minutes apart, and none are shown in your first minutes after installing the Game.

To select, deliver, limit and measure those ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects information directly from your device, including your device's advertising ID, information about your device and the Game (such as device model, operating-system version and app version), your IP address (from which an approximate, city-level location can be inferred), and your interactions with the ads shown. On Android versions that support them, it may also use Android's Privacy Sandbox advertising features, such as Topics and Attribution Reporting. The Game itself requests no location permission and receives none of this information. It is collected by Google and handled under Google's Privacy Policy and the Google Ads terms.

Consent. If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the Game shows a Google-certified consent form (built with Google's User Messaging Platform) before ads start. Personalised ads are shown only if you agree to them; otherwise you receive non-personalised or limited ads. Your choice is stored by Google's consent SDK on your device, and you can reopen the form and change your answer at any time from Settings → Ad Preferences in the Game. That setting appears only where the consent form applies.

c. Information stored only on your device

Everything the Game remembers about your play is stored in its private storage on your device and is never uploaded to us:

  • your souls, the drones you own and the drone you fly;
  • your permanent upgrades;
  • your purchases: whether you own the Starter Pack and Remove Ads, and the purchase tokens Google Play issued, which the Game keeps so that no purchase is paid out twice;
  • your control choice (touch or joystick);
  • your music and sound-effect volumes, and whether haptic feedback is on;
  • the total time the Game has been open, which is used to space out full-screen ads.

Google's consent, advertising and Firebase SDKs also keep their own settings and identifiers on your device. All of this is removed when you uninstall the Game or clear its data in your device settings.

d. Device backup

The Game switches off Android's cloud backup, so your progress is not copied to your Google account, and we never hold a copy of it. If you uninstall the Game or clear its data, your progress cannot be restored. The exception is what you bought: Google Play keeps a record of your purchases on your Google account, so Remove Ads and the Starter Pack come back after a reinstall (Soul Gem packs are used up when delivered and are not restored). On Android 12 and later, Android may still copy the Game's data to a new phone during a direct phone-to-phone transfer.

e. What the Game does not collect

To be explicit, the Game does not:

  • ask for your precise location or any location permission (Google services can infer the approximate country, region or city described in sections 2a and 2b);
  • use your camera or microphone, or access your contacts, calendar, photos or files;
  • require an account, a sign-in, a name or an email address;
  • set a Firebase user ID, or send your name, email address or other personal details to Analytics or Crashlytics;
  • see, receive or store your card or other payment details: purchases are made through Google Play, which collects them itself under its own terms;
  • show a permission prompt: every permission in section 3 is granted when you install the Game.

We may also disclose information if required by law, or to protect the rights, safety, and security of our players or the Game.

5. Data retention

Analytics and crash reports are retained according to the retention settings and terms of those Firebase services. Advertising and consent data collected by Google is retained according to Google's retention periods. Purchase records held by Google Play are kept under Google Play's terms. Data stored only on your device remains until you uninstall the Game or clear its data.

6. Your choices and data deletion

Because the Game has no accounts and no server, you control your data directly:

  • All on-device data (progress, settings, purchase tokens and ad timing) is deleted when you uninstall the Game or clear its data in your device settings. This also resets the Game's Firebase identifiers on your device, but does not delete reports that were already sent.
  • Purchases: your purchase history is kept by Google Play on your Google account, not by us, so uninstalling the Game does not delete it. You can see it in your Google Play account.
  • Advertising ID: you can reset or delete it, or turn off ad personalisation, in your device's Google settings ("Ads" under Google settings). Deleting your advertising ID stops personalised advertising.
  • Ad consent (where it applies): you can review and change your answer at any time from Settings → Ad Preferences in the Game. Clearing the Game's data also clears it, and the form asks again the next time you play.
  • If you have any other request about your data, email support@abduloski.dev and we will act on it.

7. Security

The Game's data is kept in its own private storage area, which other apps on your device cannot read. The Google SDKs it includes communicate with Google over encrypted (HTTPS) connections. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, but we take reasonable measures to limit collection, including fixed Analytics events and no Firebase user ID.

8. Children's privacy

Caldera is a general-audience game and is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe we hold information about a child, please contact us and we will act on it.

9. Your privacy rights

EEA / UK (GDPR): you have the right to access, correct, or delete your personal data, to object to or restrict certain processing, to withdraw consent, and to data portability. Our legal bases are: your consent for personalised advertising (collected through the consent form described in section 2b, and changeable at any time from Settings → Ad Preferences in the Game or in your device's Google ad settings); and our legitimate interest in keeping the Game working and improving it through analytics and crash reports, and in funding the free Game through advertising, where consent is not legally required. Where Google acts as an independent controller for advertising data, you may also exercise your rights directly with Google.

United States (California and other states with privacy laws): we do not sell your personal information, and we do not "share" it for cross-context behavioural advertising on our own behalf. You have the right to know what is collected, to request deletion, and not to be discriminated against for exercising your rights. To opt out of personalised advertising, reset or delete your advertising ID in your device's Google settings.

To exercise any of these rights, email support@abduloski.dev.

10. International data transfers

Google, the only third party involved, processes data on servers that may be located in countries other than yours, including the United States. Where required, these transfers are governed by appropriate safeguards such as the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

11. Changes to this policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time. When we do, we will revise the "Last updated" date above and, where appropriate, provide notice within the Game. Continued use of the Game after an update means you accept the revised policy.
